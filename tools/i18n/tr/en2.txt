702|17.1 Common idea of radii and chamfers on round parts
703|They all make full turns with G2 or G3, one per position, going down or up following the profile. On each turn the macro calculates the radius of the cutter center path and the Z. The smaller the step (letter H in degrees for radii, letter Q for chamfers and cones), the smoother the finish and the longer the program.
704|<b>Outside convex radius (O1635)</b>: cutter center radius = D/2 - C + C·SIN(θ) + T/2 and Z = W - C + C·COS(θ).
705|<b>Concave radius at the base (O1636)</b>: cutter center radius = D/2 + C - C·COS(φ) + T/2 and Z = Z + C - C·SIN(φ). Roughing must leave material in the corner.
706|<b>Hole chamfer (O1628)</b>: the corner of the flat end mill follows a line from the top to the wall; each turn steps Q / TAN B on the radius. Alarm 267 if C is larger than T.
707|17.2 Outside cone with an angle letter (O1630)
708|With a flat end mill any angle B from 1 to 89 degrees is possible: the cutter starts at the small diameter and goes to the large one in helical turns (letter Q sets the height of each pass). With a conical chamfer mill the angle is limited to 45 degrees to the face (alarm 286). The step left between turns can be up to Q high, so use a small Q for finishing.

710|Letters used in macros called inside other macros must not overlap: check the table before combining. When D and I are used together in the same call, test in the air and check the values.
711|An empty letter is not zero: the macro uses <code>EQ #0</code> to test it. If you want zero, write the letter with 0.
712|In all radius and chamfer macros, the cutter must have a sharp corner; a cutter with a corner radius changes the path.
713|Check the length offset of the tip (or the corner) used as reference before running.
714|18. How to test without breaking anything: first-part checklist
715|<b>Before running</b>
716|Are the O16xx subprograms loaded in memory and do they open without errors?
