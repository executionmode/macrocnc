In this lesson, we look inside the outside polygon contour macro, O sixteen seventeen.
It makes squares, hexagons, octagons: any polygon, stepping down in a spiral.

In the main program, the G sixty-five P sixteen seventeen line calls the macro. This example is a hexagon.
X and Y: part center. B: number of sides, six. D: diameter across corners, forty-one point five seven.
A: angle of the first corner, zero: corner on the X axis. E: entry distance, twenty. T: a twenty-five millimeter cutter.
W and Z: start and final Z. K: how much it goes down each lap, two. R: clearance. F: feed.
Each letter becomes a variable: B becomes variable two, D seven, A one, T twenty, and K six.

The macro starts with three simple calculations.
Variable one hundred is the corner radius: D divided by two, twenty point seven eight five. It is the distance from the center to each corner.
One oh one is the cutter radius: T divided by two, twelve point five.
And one oh two is the angle between one corner and the next: three sixty divided by B, sixty degrees.
Careful: D is measured at the corners. For a hexagon thirty-six across flats, D is thirty-six divided by cosine of thirty: forty-one point five seven.

Then the checks: fewer than three sides, K zero, or a final Z above W stop the machine, with alarms one sixty-one, one sixty-two and one sixty-three.
Here everything is fine, so the macro goes on to the entry.

The cutter comes in perpendicular to the first side, from outside the stock.
The outward direction of the first side is A minus half the corner angle: zero minus thirty, minus thirty degrees.
Variables one ten and one eleven are the entry point: the first corner, plus the cutter radius in that direction. That is X thirty-one point six one, and Y minus six point two five.
The cutter starts farther out, by E, goes down, and feeds in with G one to that point, touching the first side.

N ten starts each lap. Variable one oh three is the Z at the end of the lap: current Z minus K. On the first lap, minus two.
If it goes past the final Z, the IF holds it at the final Z.
One oh six splits that drop by the number of sides: two divided by six, zero point three three three on each side.
So the cutter does not plunge: it goes down a little on each side, in a spiral.

N twenty makes one side. Variable one oh nine is the outward direction of that side: minus thirty degrees.
The cutter runs parallel to the side, offset by its radius.
One twelve is the angle of the next corner: zero minus sixty, minus sixty. With sine and cosine, one thirteen and one fourteen give that corner's position.
The G one goes to the corner plus the cutter radius in the outward direction, going down zero point three three three.

At the corner, the cutter does not make a point: it makes a G two arc around the corner.
The arc center is the part corner itself. The G two turns sixty degrees and stops facing the next side.
I and J are minus the cutter radius times cosine and sine of the direction: the vector to the corner.
So the cutter rolls around the corner and the part corner stays sharp, with no radius.

After the arc, variable one oh eight gets the new corner, and one oh seven adds one.
The IF asks: is one oh seven still less than or equal to B? If yes, back to N twenty for the next side.
After side six, the lap is complete.

At the end of the lap, variable one oh four gets the Z reached, and the IF asks if it still went down at all.
The laps go down two at a time: zero, minus two, minus four, down to minus sixteen. The last drop only goes to minus seventeen.
Then comes a flat lap at Z minus seventeen, with one oh six equal to zero. It cleans up the spiral ramp.
With one oh six at zero, the IF does not loop again. The cutter goes up to Z minus twelve, five above the final Z, and M ninety-nine returns to the main program.

In short: corner radius, cutter radius and corner angle; the entry; the drop per side; the corner with sine and cosine; the line and the arc.
On the first part, test with W fifty and Z thirty-three: the cutter does everything in the air, with the same height.
