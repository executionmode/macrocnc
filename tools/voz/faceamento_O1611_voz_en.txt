Today we will go through the face milling macro O sixteen eleven, block by block.
It is the simplest macro in the library, and the basis for understanding all the others.

The main program calls the macro with G sixty-five P sixteen eleven.
The macro does the math and the moves, then returns with M ninety-nine.
At the machine, you only change the G sixty-five letters. Nobody touches the macro.

Each G sixty-five letter becomes a variable inside the macro.
X and Y are the size of the area: two hundred fifteen by two hundred fifteen.
W is the top of the part. Z is the final Z.
K is the depth per pass. T is the cutter diameter.
Q is the stepover. A is the clearance outside the part.
R is the clearance plane and F is the feed rate.

First block: the cutter radius.
T is fifty, so variable one hundred is fifty divided by two: twenty-five.

Now, how far the cutter center goes in X.
Half the area, one hundred seven point five, plus the radius, twenty-five, plus the clearance, five.
That gives one hundred thirty-seven point five. So the cutter leaves the part completely.

In Y, the last row is at one hundred seven point five minus twenty-five.
That gives eighty-two point five. The cutter edge stops exactly at the edge of the area.
If the cutter is bigger than the area, the IF makes a single row in the middle.

Before moving, the macro checks the data.
Z above the top, K zero, Q zero, or Q bigger than the cutter: alarms one oh one to one oh four.
Better to stop with an alarm than to cut wrong.

The current surface starts at the top: variable one oh four gets W, which is zero.
N ten: each pass goes down K. Zero minus two point five gives Z minus two point five.
The IF makes sure the cutter never goes below the final Z.

The cutter rapids to X minus one thirty-seven point five, Y minus eighty-two point five, outside the part.
It rapids down to the clearance plane: Z twenty-five.
Then it feeds down to Z minus two point five, still in the air, outside the part.

N twenty: cut in X to the opposite side, X one thirty-seven point five.
Then the sign of variable one oh six flips: the next cut comes back.
If it is not on the last row yet, add Q thirty in Y.
Y goes through minus eighty-two point five, minus fifty-two point five, minus twenty-two point five, seven point five, thirty-seven point five and sixty-seven point five.
The next one would be ninety-seven point five. That is past the limit, so the IF clamps it to eighty-two point five.
GO TO twenty repeats the cut. Seven rows in total.

N thirty: the pass is done, the cutter goes up to the clearance plane.
If the final Z is not reached yet, it goes back to N ten for the next pass.
For example, with Z minus five and K two point five, there would be two passes.
At the bottom? M ninety-nine returns to the main program.

To sum up: radius, limits, alarms, pass, back and forth, and M ninety-nine.
The first time, test with W fifty and Z forty-nine: the machine draws in the air.
And simulate first in the Macro CNC app. See you in the next macro!
